## Why this source matters

This survey separates feedback sources and evaluation questions that are often collapsed into a generic “reflection” component.

## Claims and evidence

| Kind | Claim or observation | Exact support | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| source-reported claim | The surveyed literature did not convincingly demonstrate prompted self-feedback correction outside tasks unusually suited to it, while reliable external feedback was effective. | Abstract and critical review. | Covers literature through its review period; newer models and methods require revalidation. |
| source-reported claim | Earlier studies often used ambiguous questions, impractical frameworks, or unfair evaluations that overstated self-correction. | Abstract and paper taxonomy/checklist. | A critical synthesis; individual disputed studies must be read for topic-level conclusions. |

## Evidence assessment

Peer-reviewed and methodologically focused, with high value for avoiding an anthropomorphic “reflection” bucket. Recency risk is meaningful but the feedback-source distinction is structurally durable.

## Relationships and contradictions

Qualifies positive Self-Refine and Reflexion narratives. It suggests that feedback source, error observability, critic independence, correction policy, and acceptance/rollback are separate harness decisions.

## Leads and open questions

Build a later evidence bundle including the positive systems, negative studies, program/executable feedback, and current judge-bias results.
